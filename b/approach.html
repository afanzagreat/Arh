01 / The point of it · Our approach

Personality gets attention.
Clarity earns trust.

A good website should sound, look, and work like the business it represents. Not like a theme. Not like an agency wrote it for another agency.

01 / Three things, working together

Different by design.
Useful by default.

A website can be distinctive without being confusing. The character draws people in; the useful details help them decide.

01 / RECOGNITION

Make it recognizably yours.

Good design begins with the actual business: what it does differently, how it speaks, and what its customers value.

02 / UNDERSTANDING

Make the offer easy to grasp.

People should not have to decode clever headlines to learn what you offer, who it helps, or where you work.

03 / ACTION

Make the next step feel simple.

On a phone or a big screen, the important information and the right action should be easy to find.

02 / A two-minute first-screen check

Give your homepage a fair test.

Open it on your phone and look at the first screen before you scroll. Can a new visitor answer these three questions without guessing?

  1. What does this business do?If the headline could belong to almost any company, make the offer more specific.
  2. Is this for someone like me?Name the service, location, customer, or situation that makes the fit obvious.
  3. What is the sensible next step?Give the visitor one clear action—call, book, ask, or visit—without a scavenger hunt.

03 / Trust, without the theatre

Trust is shown in the details.
Not claimed in bigger adjectives.

Useful proof beats empty boasting: a clear explanation, honest expectations, real contact details, and terms you can understand before saying yes. If a project needs a claim or credential, it should be one the business can actually stand behind.

04 / How the work is judged

A few non-negotiables.

Style matters. So does the part you notice when you actually try to use the site.

01 / MADE FOR YOU

No theme-first thinking.

The visual direction should come from the business and its customers, not a template's limitations.

02 / PHONE-FIRST

Comfortable on a small screen.

Readable type, sensible spacing, and actions people can reach without pinching or hunting.

03 / PLAIN TERMS

Know what is included.

Agree the scope and the ongoing terms before work begins. Ask questions before you commit.

04 / NO PRESSURE

Take a proper look.

Every website starts with a 7-day review period, with clear terms and room to think.
